Memory gives a place its meaning.

The people behind the place

It began with
a story worth hearing.

Family memories helped bring Mount Tabor’s history into view.

The Gumby family’s stories and the Ward/Smith family’s connection to the cemetery helped neighbors see what this place holds. Their memories are a starting point for understanding the people, faith and community that shaped it.

Discover the church and cemetery ↗

One place. Many lives.

More than a building.
A community’s history.

Mount Tabor’s nineteenth-century church and cemetery connect us to Black community life in Mount Holly Springs after the Civil War.

The church

A setting for worship, gathering and shared community life.

The cemetery

A place of remembrance, family connections and the lives of United States Colored Troops veterans.

Make a connection with this place ↗

Care makes continuity possible

A remarkable journey.
Continuing work.

Community commitment and preservation expertise have helped the church endure. Its history is recognized; its care continues.

2018

Recognition of a place at risk

Preservation Pennsylvania included Mount Tabor on its At Risk list.

2021

A nationally recognized history

The church and cemetery were listed together in the National Register of Historic Places.

2024

Preservation work honored

The project received a Pennsylvania Historic Preservation Rehabilitation Award.

Published priorities / 2026

What comes next

The project’s published plans include ongoing maintenance, improved access and facilities, interpretation and tours.

  • Care for the church and cemetery
  • Work toward access and facility improvements
  • Help more people encounter the history
