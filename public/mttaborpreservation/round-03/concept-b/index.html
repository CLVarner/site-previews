Mount Holly Springs, Pennsylvania

A place worth remembering.

Mt. Tabor AME Zion Church and its cemetery hold a Black community’s history, cared for by the people connected to it.

One site, two histories

The church and the burial ground.

Built for worship, gathering and shared community life.

A nineteenth-century church at the centre of Black community life in Mount Holly Springs after the Civil War.

The cemetery

A place of remembrance.

Family connections and the lives of United States Colored Troops veterans are recorded here. The project does not publish a burial count, and uncertain cemetery locations are not identified.

How the site is cared for ↗

The people behind the place

Memory gives a place its meaning.

Family memories from the Gumby family and the Ward/Smith family’s connection to the cemetery helped neighbours see what this place holds. Those memories are a starting point for understanding the people, faith and community who shaped it.
