The house’s history

Stone walls.
A different sense of place.

Built around 1820, Two Mile House once offered travelers a meal and a place to stay along Walnut Bottom Road. It operated as a tavern from 1826 to 1857 before becoming a private residence.

The house’s limestone walls, Georgian floor plan and Federal details belong to that early history. The Moore family added the terrace room in the 1920s, giving the building another layer of character.

In 1946, Mary Wheeler King and J. McLain King purchased the property. It was their home for decades, and both were active supporters of Cumberland County Historical Society. Mary was the first woman elected to the Society’s board.

1820

A historic CCHS property

Entrusted to CCHS in 1992.

Mary Wheeler King bequeathed Two Mile House to Cumberland County Historical Society in 1992, with the wish that it remain open for public and private events. The house was listed on the National Register of Historic Places that same year.

Public programs began in 1994. Today, weddings and other gatherings continue here alongside its role as a historic CCHS property. Your rental covers the entire property; catering and other event services are arranged separately.
