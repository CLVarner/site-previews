Hands-on learning in the Odd Fellows proposal.

Educational objects, historical dress, and traditional trades give people practical ways to encounter history. The Odd Fellows proposal would provide classroom and woodworking spaces to support that work.

History through practical participation.

The Society's educational work brings people into contact with educational objects, historical garments, and trades. Trying on historical dress or exploring how a traditional skill works offers another way to approach the past alongside reading and looking.

Classes in the Reading Room and opportunities to explore historical dress at a Block Party are part of the Society's existing educational work. The campaign would support that practical approach through spaces planned for Odd Fellows.

Spaces suited to hands-on learning.

The Odd Fellows proposal includes a flexible classroom and woodworking space on the first floor. These rooms would support educational activity, including field trips and workshops, while restoration cares for the building itself.

Different activities need different working spaces. A classroom would provide a setting for learning together; woodworking space would support practical encounters with a traditional trade. The campaign connects those uses with care for a historic place.

Part of the wider proposal.

Education is one role in the Odd Fellows plan, alongside staff work and a restored hall for lectures and community events. The project page explains the proposed floor-by-floor uses and exterior elevator.

The wider campaign also includes collection and research improvements and a new visitor arrival. Those investments support the care and discovery of historical materials; education connects that work with people encountering the county's history.
